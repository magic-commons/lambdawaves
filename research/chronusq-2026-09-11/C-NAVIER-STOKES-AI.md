# C-NAVIER-STOKES-AI — fact check: "OpenAI also solved Navier–Stokes"

Date of this check: 2026-09-11. Status marks used below: **PROVEN** (community-accepted, published) / **NUMERICAL** (no rigor claimed) / **ANNOUNCED** (claimed, Lean/CAP-checked by the claimant, not yet independently ratified) / **RETRACTED** / **UNVERIFIED** (I could not open a primary source).

## Bottom line (see full correction in part d)

Two *different*, real, recent events are almost certainly being merged into one. (1) DeepMind + academic co-authors published a **numerical** discovery (PINN-based) of new *unstable* self-similar blow-up profiles for model equations (IPM, Boussinesq, 3D Euler-with-boundary) in September 2025 — not Navier–Stokes, not a proof, not Millennium-eligible. (2) OpenAI announced on 2026-09-08 an **AI-generated proof, formalized in Lean**, that the actual 3D Navier–Stokes equations *can* develop a finite-time singularity — but only in the **forced** case (Clay's official alternatives C/D, which explicitly permit an external smooth force), not the unforced case (A/B) that most mathematicians consider the "real" open question. As of 2026-09-10/11 the Clay Mathematics Institute still lists Navier–Stokes as unsolved (their rules require publication, a ≥2-year waiting period, and community consensus before any prize is paid), and the announcement is entangled in a credit/priority dispute with NYU's Tristan Buckmaster and Anthropic's Levent Alpöge. There is no general-purpose "super math" here that can just be pointed at another problem — the content is a highly specific, hand-built fluid-dynamics construction plus an agentic-scaling story.

## (b) The mathematics, in plain equations

**The Clay problem itself.** Navier–Stokes on R³, viscosity ν>0, external force f, initial data u₀:
```
∂t u + (u·∇)u − ν∆u + ∇p = f,      ∇·u = 0,      u(x,0) = u₀(x)
```
Fefferman's official four alternatives:
- **(A)/(B):** f ≡ 0 (no force). Prove smooth u₀ with finite energy always yields a smooth solution for all time, on R³ (A) or the periodic torus T³ (B). — **still fully open**, and this is the version most people mean by "did anyone solve Navier–Stokes."
- **(C)/(D):** f is allowed to be smooth (but nonzero). Exhibit *any* smooth divergence-free u₀ and smooth f for which no global smooth solution exists (i.e., construct breakdown). — this is what both the Buckmaster–Alpöge and OpenAI results target, and it is explicitly Clay-legal, not a loophole.

**OpenAI's theorem (their own PDF, Theorem 1.1):** for every ν>0 they construct a compactly-supported smooth force f, a compact set K, and smooth (u,p) on R³×[0,1) with u(·,0)=0, such that
```
sup_{0≤t<1} ‖u(t)‖_{L²(R³)} < ∞      but      limsup_{t↑1} ‖u(t)‖_{L∞(R³)} = ∞
```
i.e. bounded kinetic energy, unbounded velocity — a finite-time singularity, "establishing alternative (C)" (and, via compact support, (D) on T³ too). Physically: a self-similar vortex core that spirals inward and stretches "like spaghetti," shrinking and speeding up while total energy stays finite; the construction's technical burden is arranging that the *individual* terms of the residual force blow up while their *sum* stays smooth — done via layered oscillatory corrections stacked on the Córdoba–Martínez-Zoroa cascade idea (successive amplification of increasingly concentrated vortex/strain layers, with each layer's self-interaction cancelled by construction), building on Tao's averaged-Navier–Stokes blowup, Buckmaster–Vicol's convex-integration non-uniqueness, and Albritton–Brué–Colombo's non-uniqueness examples.

**The DeepMind "Discovery of Unstable Singularities" paper (arXiv:2509.14185) is a different, earlier, narrower thing.** It searches for **self-similar blow-up profiles** of model equations (IPM, 2D Boussinesq, 3D Euler-with-boundary — *not* Navier–Stokes), using the classic self-similar ansatz
```
u(x,t) = (T−t)^{−α} U(x/(T−t)^{β}, ...)
```
which converts a time-evolving blow-up search into finding a *stationary* profile U at a specific scaling exponent λ ("admissible" λ). Their contribution: a **physics-informed neural network (PINN)** representing U, trained with a **full-matrix Gauss–Newton optimizer** (not first-order Adam/SGD) to drive the PDE residual down to as low as O(10⁻¹³) — "near double-float machine precision," which they state is the precision level required to feed a subsequent rigorous computer-assisted proof (interval arithmetic), though the paper itself performs no such proof. Crucially, they systematically find not just the known *stable* singularity but a whole discrete ladder of increasingly **unstable** ones (n = 1, 2, 3, … unstable directions), and observe a clean empirical asymptotic relation between the scaling exponent λ_n and the instability order n. This generalizes the pattern already established rigorously for the boundary/Euler case by Chen & Hou's 2022 computer-assisted proof of the Hou–Luo scenario, and sits alongside Elgindi's 2023 proof of finite-time singularity for C¹,ᵅ (non-smooth) Euler solutions and Buckmaster–Vicol's 2019 convex-integration non-uniqueness proof — three genuinely **proven** results, but each for a different, more tractable variant (boundary, low regularity, or non-uniqueness rather than blow-up-from-smooth-data) than the full smooth unforced 3D Navier–Stokes problem.

**What's proven vs numerical, summarized:**
- Chen–Hou (2022): PROVEN (CAP) — Euler-with-boundary / Boussinesq.
- Elgindi (2023): PROVEN — Euler, C¹,ᵅ data.
- Buckmaster–Vicol (2019): PROVEN — non-uniqueness of weak solutions.
- DeepMind "Discovery of Unstable Singularities" (2025): NUMERICAL — new unstable profiles, IPM/Boussinesq/Euler-boundary, no proof attached.
- Buckmaster–Alpöge (2026-08): ANNOUNCED, Lean-formalized — forced Euler/Boussinesq/IPM, not Navier–Stokes.
- OpenAI (2026-09-08): ANNOUNCED, Lean-formalized by OpenAI itself — forced 3D Navier–Stokes, resolving Clay's (C)/(D); independent verification and Clay recognition are both still pending.

## (c) Transfer verdict for λWAVES

The owner's instinct — "use whatever super math they got" — doesn't cash out the way it's phrased, because there is no reusable "Navier–Stokes solving algorithm" to import; it's a bespoke fluid construction. But the DeepMind *methodology* (not the OpenAI fluids proof) does contain a genuinely transferable pattern for a WebGPU quantum-chemistry / chemical-kinetics instrument:

**Transferable — the saddle/unstable-manifold viewpoint.** DeepMind's framing (find a stationary profile parametrized by admissible λ, classify each solution by its *number of unstable directions* n, expect a discrete ladder λ₁ > λ₂ > λ₃ … of increasingly unstable/higher-energy solutions) is structurally identical to searching a potential energy surface (PES) for critical points classified by Morse index: index-0 = local minimum (stable), **index-1 saddle = transition state** (exactly one negative Hessian eigenvalue = exactly one unstable direction), index-≥2 = higher-order saddles relevant to bifurcating reaction pathways. "One more unstable direction per solution" in the fluids paper is the same bookkeeping as "one more imaginary vibrational frequency per higher-index saddle" in chemistry. This is a legitimate, honest transfer: adopting the discipline of (i) representing a candidate stationary state with a differentiable ansatz, (ii) driving the governing-equation/energy-gradient residual toward machine precision with a second-order optimizer (Gauss–Newton/natural-gradient/Levenberg–Marquardt, not plain SGD) rather than stopping at "good enough," and (iii) explicitly diagonalizing the linearization at the candidate to *count and certify* its instability index before trusting it as a transition state — is a sound, importable engineering practice for λWAVES' PES/transition-state search or for finding unstable fixed points/limit cycles in a chemical-kinetics model.

**Transferable, with real effort — the residual/CAP discipline.** The idea that "residual near double-float machine precision is the threshold that makes a rigorous computer-assisted proof feasible" generalizes: if λWAVES ever wants to *certify* (not just numerically locate) a stationary electronic state, a reaction-rate fixed point, or a limit cycle, the relevant tool is the same family of techniques used in Chen–Hou-style CAPs — Newton–Kantorovich/radii-polynomial arguments with interval arithmetic bounding the linearized operator's inverse near the numerical candidate. This is real, standard, and used well outside fluids (dynamical systems, celestial mechanics, some PDE eigenvalue problems) — but it is a heavy, problem-specific undertaking each time, not something obtained by pointing at OpenAI's fluids paper.

**Not transferable — the fluid-specific ansatz and construction.** The self-similar rescaling u(x,t)=(T−t)^{−α}U(x/(T−t)^β), the compact-support/oscillatory-layer cascade trick that keeps the *forcing* smooth while individual terms blow up, and the vortex/spaghetti geometric picture are all specific to the scaling symmetry of Euler/Navier–Stokes/IPM/Boussinesq. Electronic structure and chemical-kinetics equations don't have a finite-time blow-up structure or that scaling symmetry, so none of that machinery ports over — there is no "Schrödinger-equation analogue" of a self-similar collapsing vortex waiting to be found in this work.

**Honest bottom line for λWAVES:** worth adopting — the Morse-index/unstable-direction framing for transition-state search, and the "push residuals to machine precision with a second-order optimizer, then diagonalize to certify the index" discipline. Not worth chasing — the actual fluid-dynamics content of either the DeepMind or OpenAI papers; there is nothing chemistry-specific in them to copy.

## (d) One-paragraph correction for the owner

What actually happened is two different things getting merged into one headline. Google DeepMind (with academic co-authors, including a couple of names that also show up in the OpenAI story) published a *numerical* paper in September 2025 that found new, more unstable blow-up shapes for a few simplified fluid equations using a neural network trained to extremely high precision — impressive engineering, but explicitly not a proof and not about Navier–Stokes. Separately, on 2026-09-08, OpenAI announced that an internal, unreleased AI model produced an actual proof, checked in the formal-verification language Lean, that the *real* Navier–Stokes equations can blow up — but only in the version where you're allowed to add a smooth external push (a legal but "easier" corner of the official problem, not the version most mathematicians care about, which is whether unforced turbulence can ever go infinite on its own — that's still open). The announcement landed in the middle of a nasty credit dispute with an NYU professor and an Anthropic researcher who'd been quietly doing very similar work for a year, and the Clay Institute — which pays the $1 million prize — still lists the problem as unsolved and won't even consider it until it's published, aged two years, and accepted by the math community. So: real event, real (if narrow and disputed) mathematics, not yet a verified "solved," and definitely not a general-purpose tool that can be pointed at an unrelated project — it's a bespoke fluid-dynamics construction, not "super math" in a bottle.
