## About-page references

**Chronus Quantum.** The credited project is ChronusQ, a computational electronic-structure package supporting time-dependent simulations, not a single universal “molecular orbital model.” Its paper and RT documentation are appropriate scientific references. Current Lambda Waves code implements its own reduced models; no ChronusQ dependency or numerical equivalence was established. Credit it as inspiration, and cite a specific method when a matched implementation exists. [Package paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.01381); [RT documentation](https://github.com/xsligroup/chronusq_public/wiki/RT).

**Brian Johnson's Electron Orbitals.** The developer's Play Store listing describes an atomic orbital visualiser with higher-n states. It supports the visual/interaction inspiration, not an inference that the app solves molecular correlation or driven charge migration. [Developer listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.gputreats.orbitalexplorer).

**Paul Falstad.** His molecular viewer explicitly illustrates the one-electron H₂⁺ ion. Its directions distinguish molecular combinations and time-dependent states; it is a relevant small-model reference. His atomic dipole-transition applet supplies a separate driven-transition example. Neither should be cited as a complete arbitrary-molecule solver. [Molecular viewer](https://www.falstad.com/qmmo/); [directions](https://www.falstad.com/qmmo/directions.html); [atomic dipole transitions](https://www.falstad.com/qmatomrad/).

**Seth Shultz and Beatriz Erranté / JSY–Finite Field programme.** Preserved as internal research attribution. No external paper URL is supplied by the About page, and their names are not evidence of an independently verified molecular method. No attempt was made to infer or investigate private personal information.

The MOrbVis paper supports the corpus's claimed publication and reports sub-100 ms evaluation in its benchmark configurations. Those figures were not reproduced locally and are not Lambda Waves performance guarantees. Its README currently lists s/p/d/f support while the paper describes s–g; pin and test the actual implementation version before designing an importer around g-shell support.

Research breadth here includes exact amplitude mapping, coherent-state geometry, moving bases, variational forces, real-time HF/DFT, parameterised tight binding, correlated active spaces, nuclear branching, Coulomb regularisation, continuum solvers and GPU orbital rendering. Additional bibliography in the original dossier remains a reading backlog, not an endorsed list of verified claims.
