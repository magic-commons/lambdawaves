Dear Professor Berry,

Your 2001 paper on knotted zeros in the quantum states of hydrogen is the whole starting point of the short paper attached, and I would be grateful
if you would read it before it goes anywhere public. It was written by an AI system (Claude, "Fable 5.1", with a second AI model doing the
mathematics and the checks) working with me on λWAVES, an instrument that plays hydrogen superpositions in real time; the paper says so plainly.

**What it does.** It rebuilds your construction in the instrument's conventions (Condon–Shortley phases, the modern Laguerre normalisation) and
reproduces your printed digits; in doing so it finds a misprint in your equation 4. It then moves the twist from the unperturbed state into the
perturbation, which lowers every torus knot: a trefoil and a Hopf link at n = 4 (your trefoil needed n = 7), the cinquefoil at n = 5, and in general
n_G(p, q) = min_a max(a + 2p, |q − a| + 1) against n_B = min(q + 2p, p + 2q). It also shows that level 2 holds no knot, and that the polynomial
knots of optics multiplied by e^{−r/n} are not hydrogen states unless the polynomial is harmonic and homogeneous of degree n − 1.

**Verification.** The knots are traced on the zero set and identified by Alexander polynomials and braid words; the oracle is the instrument's own
wavefunction evaluator to 5e-15. The coefficients are printed in the paper so anyone can redo it.

**What is not verified.** No independent re-computation by a human; one reader (me); the forward-citation search on your paper (and on Enciso, Hartley and Peralta-Salas 2018) was done on 25 October through
OpenAlex with keyword and web searches, found no hydrogen knot below your levels, and has not yet covered the arXiv full text or your appendices with
Dennis. The mechanism is not claimed as new: moving the twist into the perturbation is the structure of the Milnor polynomials of optical vortex knots
(Dennis et al. 2010); the paper claims only that it works inside one hydrogen level. The status table says exactly this.

**The one question.** Is the n = 4 trefoil, or the lowering rule, already known to you or in work I have missed? If so I would rather cite than claim.

Thank you, and thank you for the 2001 paper.
