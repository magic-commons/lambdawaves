## 2. The technique, named and explained

**Best single hypothesis [I]: a *displaced line family accumulated additively*, i.e. a fold-caustic of a 1-parameter family of curves.** Take a field of thin white lines (grid, parallel lines, or the bright pixels of an input picture). Push every point by a smooth displacement D(x) = A·n(k·x) (noise, a lens-like refraction, or a "light bending" rule). Draw the pushed lines with **additive** blending into a float buffer with low per-line intensity, many lines (or many jittered copies of the same lines). The result's brightness at pixel y is the density ρ(y) = Σ_{preimages x} w / |det(I + ∇D(x))|. Where the Jacobian det vanishes (A·k·|n'| ≈ 1) neighbouring lines land on the same pixels: ρ diverges as |d|^-1/2 — that *is* a fold caustic, identical in maths to light refracted by a wavy surface. Below threshold the lines only wobble (coherent lineart); as A·k passes 1 they fold into smooth bright sheets with cusps; as the sample count rises the individual strands vanish into a continuous "caustic" field. This matches his gallery text ("how light bends or accumulates", "edges soften or dissolve", "nothing here was ever lit"), his 2023 "Caustics"/"Line lights" captions, and his shader-at-the-pixel-level practice.
Decoherence knob = **warp strength × warp frequency**, smoothness knob = **number of accumulated lines/samples** (noise per bin ~ 1/√N), softness = **line width vs line spacing**, look = **exposure/tone map** on the float buffer.

Alternatives, in order of likelihood:
1. **Refraction light-transport (vertex/grid pass)**: a grid of parallel "rays" refracted through a height field, projected to a floor and summed, intensity = oldArea/newArea (Wallace/Heckel, §3C). Same maths; the "lines" are the ray grid seen before refraction. Fits "lenticular lenses… rebuilt in code".
2. **Sand/point splatting along lines (Hoff/Tarbell, §3A–B)**: thousands of 1-px alpha dots along each curve, curves random-walked over time; density modulates with curve speed/length. Produces exactly the soft caustic-like mass; cheap on GPU as a point pass.
3. **Fragment-only stochastic gather**: per pixel, N taps of the line image at p − D_k(p), weighted by 1/|det J|; approximates the same density field without a point pass.
4. **Feedback + displace (TouchDesigner style)**: previous frame × 0.99, blurred, displaced by slow noise, plus fresh lines → trails/afterimages ("luminous forms leave trails"). Likely used for the animated pieces, not for the still folds.
5. Per-channel (RGB) offsets: three displacement amplitudes (or three refraction indices) accumulated into R, G, B give the chromatic fringes he shows; title "Chromatic Geometries" supports this. **[I]**

## 3. Algorithms from others (numbered recipes)

**A. Tarbell "SandPainter" (Substrate 2003/2004) [V from the xscreensaver port, which "directly ported" `substrate_s.pde`; the Processing.js mirror confirms the constructor/gain]**
1. State: colour c; gain g = random(0.01, 0.1).
2. Each stroke between crack point o and far point p: g += random(−0.05, 0.05), clamp to [0, 1]; grains = 64; w = g/(grains−1).
3. For i in 0..grains−1: alpha = 0.1 − i/(grains·10) (0.1 → ~0.0016); t = sin(sin(i·w)) (port: sin(sandp + sin(i·w)), sandp = 0); plot point o + (p−o)·t with that alpha, alpha-blended (not clamped additive).
4. Same 64 grains regardless of stroke length → short strokes dense, long strokes faint; sin(sin()) crowds grains near the origin. Sand Stroke page: "deposits precisely the same number of sand grains despite the magnitude of the wave… a very interesting density modulation across an even mass of sand."
Sources: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Zygo/xscreensaver/master/hacks/substrate.c , https://math.vu.nl/~eliens/demo/javascript-processing-example-custom-substrate.htm , http://www.complexification.net/gallery/machines/sandstroke/ , https://discourse.processing.org/t/trying-to-understand-the-substrate-algorithm/3031

**B. Anders Hoff "Sand Spline" / "grains of sand" [V from repo code + blog via proxy]**
1. A B-spline with pnum control nodes (hlines example: 100 rows, row i has 4+i nodes, canvas 5000², unit coords, EDGE 0.08).
2. Each iteration every node gets noise[k] += U(−1,1)·scale[k], scale[k] = k·STP (STP = 0.0000003·0.15 ≈ 4.5e-8/step, ramps along the curve so the first node stays put); node moves along a fresh random unit direction by noise[k]. Nodes random-walk, so the curve "gradually become[s] more erratic".
3. Re-interpolate the curve to INUM = 1000 points, paint them as 1-px dots with rgba (0,1,1,0.001) — alpha 0.001 — on white; write PNG with GAMMA 1.5 every 50 000 iterations.
4. His words: "Instead of simply drawing the spline, I draw a series of transparent dots along the spline for every iteration. I also make sure to draw the same number of dots each time" → density ∝ 1/speed of the curve. Grains of sand: "drawing several, almost transparent, dots at random along a line"; keep colour/alpha as **floats 0–1, not uint8**, or faint grains round to nothing.
Sources: https://inconvergent.net/generative/sand-spline (403 direct; read via proxy), https://inconvergent.net/2017/grains-of-sand/ , https://github.com/inconvergent/sand-spline (main-hlines.py, modules/sandSpline.py), https://github.com/inconvergent/fast-sand-paint , https://github.com/inconvergent/sand-glyphs

**C. Refraction caustics as area ratio [V]** (Evan Wallace WebGL Water; Maxime Heckel R3F)
1. Mesh/grid of rays from the light; per vertex: ray = refract(−light, normal, IOR_air/IOR_water); oldPos = project(flat), newPos = project(displaced).
2. Rasterise the mesh at newPos; fragment: oldArea = |dFdx(oldPos)|·|dFdy(oldPos)|, newArea likewise; intensity = oldArea/newArea·0.2 ("if the triangle gets smaller, it gets brighter"). Heckel: value = clamp(ratio,0,1)·uIntensity; value *= value; blend One / SrcAlpha; IOR 1/1.25; 2000² target.
3. Chromatic fringe (Heckel): per-channel UV offsets uAberration·slide·{1,2,3} over 16 samples with alternating sign, then saturate 1.265. Physically: three IORs.
Sources: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/evanw/webgl-water/master/renderer.js , https://blog.maximeheckel.com/posts/caustics-in-webgl/ ; fold/cusp theory (Jacobian zero set, 1-to-1 outside, 3-to-1 inside) https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0505658

**D. Flow fields (Hobbs) [V]**: angle grid at 0.5 % of width, angle = map(noise,0,1,0,2π), noise step ≈ 0.005, step length 0.1–0.5 % width; "when you use continuous distortion, your curves will not cross" — so flow fields alone give coherent lines; folds need an explicit displacement, not advection. https://www.tylerxhobbs.com/words/flow-fields

**E. Domain warp (Quilez) [V]**: q = (fbm(p), fbm(p+(5.2,1.3))); r = (fbm(p+4q+(1.7,9.2)), fbm(p+4q+(8.3,2.8))); f = fbm(p+4r); colour from f, |q|, r.y — the standard way to build a smooth D(x) and to get free palette indices. https://iquilezles.org/articles/warp/

**F. Splatting**: "SandSim: curve-guided Gaussian splatting for reconstructing sand painting" (2025/26 paper; page 403'd, title only) https://gamedev.net/news/3064-sandsim-curve-guided-gaussian-splatting-for-reconstructing-sand-painting/ ; Hoff's method is already 1-px splatting; 2-D Gaussian splats just replace the 1-px dot with a σ-wide kernel.

**G. TouchDesigner feedback chain [V]**: Feedback → Level opacity 0.99 → Blur → Transform (−0.01 Y) → Displace weight 0.01 with slow low-contrast noise; Derivative tutorials add Edge/Lookup/Bloom. https://github.com/LucieMrc/TD_feedback_love_EN , https://derivative.ca/community-post/tutorial/displaced-noise-touchdesigner-tutorial-15/71162 . ## 4. Colour and palette

- Where the RGB comes from in these looks: (i) per-channel displacement/IOR offsets → fringes at every fold (Heckel ×1,2,3; physically dispersion); (ii) additive white saturating: low-intensity tinted samples sum toward white at the caustic cores (Hoff's cyan at α 0.001 reads as cyan→white); (iii) hue cycling — Lieberman explicitly avoids it.
- Palette-LUT replacement: accumulate **scalars, not colour**. Buffer channels: ρ (density), ρ·u (u = phase: arc-length along line, iteration index, or source-line id), optionally ρ₁,ρ₂,ρ₃ for three displacement scales. Final pass: I = tone(ρ) with tone(ρ)=1−exp(−k·ρ) or (ρ/ρ_max)^γ; colour = LUT(I) (density→LUT, "sand" look) or LUT(mean phase) scaled by I (arrival-order→LUT, Hoff "sand creatures" look) or Σ_i LUT_i(tone(ρ_i)) with three palette primaries replacing R,G,B (keeps the chromatic fringe but in the palette). Quilez's q/r intermediates give a second LUT axis for free.
- Keep everything linear float; apply gamma (Hoff 1.5) or filmic only at the end; LUT sampled with linear filtering, 256×1.

## 5. Recommended GPU node (two variants)

Input: picture (bright lines on black) or a line list. Output: tone-mapped, palette-coloured caustic field. Shared: displacement field D(x,seed) = A·R(s)·[fbm₂(f·x + seed₁), fbm₂(f·x + seed₂)] (or curl noise for divergence-free), 2–4 octaves; chromatic spread δ gives A_c = A·(1 + δ·c), c ∈ {−1,0,1}.

**(a) Fragment-only gather (one pass, no geometry)** — per pixel p: for k in 0..N−1 (N = 16–64, blue-noise/hash seeds): x_k = p − D(p + j_k) with jitter j_k ~ σ_line; weight w_k = 1/max(|det(I + ∇D(x_k))|, ε) (∇D by two extra fbm evals or from a precomputed D texture's finite differences); ρ += w_k · L(x_k) (L = luminance of input, optionally 1-px dilated). Divide by N. Cost: 1080p × N=32 ≈ 66 M taps + noise evals; fine at 60 fps in WGSL. Temporal variant: accumulate 1 tap per frame into a float history buffer with decay d (the afterimage/trail look). Limitation: a gather at p only finds one preimage; the Jacobian weight restores the fold brightness approximately; true multi-preimage overlap needs variant (b).

**(b) Point-splat pass (true forward density)** — 1. Build a sample set once per input change: M points on the lines (importance-sample bright pixels into a storage buffer via prefix-sum, or sample polylines at Δs); each carries u (arc-length/line id). 2. Compute shader: for each point and each of S copies (S = 1–16), y = x + D(x + j, seed_s), per channel c; atomicAdd fixed-point (e.g. 16.16 u32) into three density planes + one phase plane at floor(y), or bilinear 4-tap splat; optional Gaussian splat radius σ via a small loop. M·S ≈ 2–8 M splats/frame is comfortable in WebGPU. (WebGL fallback: POINTS with gl.blendFunc(ONE, ONE) into RGBA16F/32F; point size = σ.) 3. Resolve pass: ρ_c → tone → LUT as in §4; optional 1-px blur for 1/√N grain. This is Hoff/Tarbell on the GPU and produces real folds, cusps and caustic sheets.

Knobs (8): **warp amplitude A** (0 → coherent lines, A·f·|n'| ≈ 1 → first folds, 3–10× → smooth caustic); **warp frequency f** (and octaves); **samples N / copies S** (grainy → smooth); **line/splat width σ**; **exposure k** (tone-map scale; raises cores to white); **gamma/curve** (1.5–2.2); **chromatic spread δ** (0 = mono, 0.02–0.1 = fringes); **palette LUT + index mode** (density / phase / 3-primary). Extra: temporal decay d for trails; seed.
