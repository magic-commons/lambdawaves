## WHAT THE LOOK IS (the short version; RESEARCH.md has the long one)
White lines on black that "decohere" into smooth caustic sheets is a SCATTER, not a blur: every bright point of the lines is moved by a smooth displacement field (noise) and DEPOSITED additively where it lands (a splat). Where the displacement map folds (its Jacobian vanishes) the deposits pile up — those bright folds ARE caustics, the same singularities a wavy water surface throws on the pool floor. Small displacement = the lines, softened; large = the lines dissolve into sheets; few samples per point = grain (Monte-Carlo noise, the film look Josh loves), many = smooth. Per-channel (R · G · B) displacement with slightly different fields gives Lieberman's colour fringes. The pieces are the Heft Gallery show "What Light May Be" (NYC, 14 Oct–13 Nov 2026: Nine Unique Lightfields, Chromatic Geometries, the Lightfield Studies prints from his daily sketches; RESEARCH.md §1, the images themselves were not reachable — the method is inferred from his words: light on metal and lenticular film "rebuilt in code, exaggerated past what physical materials allow", forms "lines, grids, boxes, curves", "edges soften or dissolve", "how light bends or accumulates"). The transition is governed by amplitude × frequency: A·f·|n′| ≈ 1 is the first fold, 3–10× is the smooth sheet (§2); the tone map is 1 − exp(−k·ρ). A gather (a fragment shader warping the picture) cannot do this — it preserves brightness per pixel, not mass — so the node is a SPLAT PASS: a point per source sample, drawn additively, then tone-mapped through a palette.

## THE ARCHITECTURE (where it lives)
NODES (`docs/laws/nodes.md`) compiles a column of operators over the stage picture into the kit's one SVG picture filter (TABLE · MATRIX fold, PRIMITIVE is a pass) and DOM LAYERS over the stage (`lab/node-layers.js`, `#nodeLayers`, z 8). A generative pass over the picture fits none of those (Josh's first bullet) — so 024 adds a fifth class, **SHADER**: a real GPU pass over the stage picture on the field's own WebGPU device, drawn into its own canvas in `#nodeLayers`. The test node is **DECOHERE**.
- THE TAP (the only `lab/field.js` change): `field.tap(fn | null)`. With a tap set, the canvas is configured with `usage: RENDER_ATTACHMENT | COPY_SRC`; after `encodeRender` in `frame()` (:2002) the encoder copies the current texture into a kept tap texture (same format, recreated on a size change, `TEXTURE_BINDING | COPY_DST`) in the SAME submit, then `fn(tapTexture, w, h)` runs after the submit. No WGSL changes, no pixel changes: THE LOCK's `readPixels` renders its own texture and never sees the tap.
- THE SHADER HOST (`lab/node-shader.js`, new, the whole class: ≤ 260 lines incl. WGSL): one canvas per SHADER node inside `#nodeLayers` in graph order (the layer host delegates a `gpu` type to it: `createLayerHost` gets the shader host handed in, builds a `<canvas class="node-layer">` instead of a div, same order/removal rules), `getContext('webgpu')` on the field's `device`, `alphaMode: 'premultiplied'`, size = the stage canvas's width/height, CSS inset 0. It draws on every tap (a present) and on every knob change from the last tap texture (kept), never on its own timer: a still instrument is still; motion is PHASE routed from a MIR LFO, as any dial.
- THE PASSES: (1) SPLAT — `point-list` draw, instanced over the source grid (W/STEP × H/STEP points × SAMPLES copies); the vertex shader reads the tap at its grid cell (luma; EDGES mode: a 3 × 3 Sobel, 4 extra reads), discards a dark point (position off-screen), displaces by a smooth noise field d(p, seed) × AMOUNT (px) with a per-sample seed jitter (SPREAD = how different the copies' fields are: 0 = all copies coincide, the pure fold; 1 = each copy its own field = the soft wash), per channel a seed offset × FRINGE; writes weight/SAMPLES; fragment adds into an `rgba16float` target (blend one/one). (2) TONE — a full-screen pass: density × GAIN → tone (1 − exp(−x), the accumulation's natural knee) → COLOUR: PICTURE (the splat carried the source's rgb) · PALETTE (density → the app's current palette LUT, a 256 × 1 texture, refreshed when the palette changes; with FRINGE > 0 the three channel densities are coloured by three palette picks (the LUT at ⅙ · ½ · ⅚) and summed, so the fringe stays inside the palette — RESEARCH.md §4's three-primary road — instead of raw R · G · B) · INK (white, the Lieberman look) → the node's canvas, premultiplied, black where nothing landed (the node REPLACES the picture under it: an opaque black ground).
- THE KNOBS (MIR controls, every number a modulation target `nodes.<node>.<knob>`): AMOUNT 0–120 px (home 24; 0 = the lines as they are, the neutral: emits nothing), SCALE (noise wavelength 20–600 px, log, home 160), SAMPLES 1–32 (home 8; the cost knob), SPREAD 0–1 (home 0.35), FRINGE 0–1 (home 0.25), GAIN 0.1–8 (log, home 1), PHASE 0–1 (the seed's drift; the motion knob), SOURCE: PICTURE · EDGES (home EDGES), COLOUR: INK · PICTURE · PALETTE (home PALETTE — Josh: "let palette control"). STEP (the grid pitch) is not a knob: 2 px, 1 under 1 Mpx, 3 above 4 Mpx.
- THE COST IS ON THE CARD: class mark `····`, HEAVY; the foot counts a SHADER as a pass. The compiler treats `cls: 'shader'` as a LAYER for ordering (no FADE/MASK), listed among `layers` with a `gpu` flag.
- THE FILTER: the kit's CSS filter dresses the stage canvas only; a SHADER canvas mirrors it (`style.filter` copied when the stage canvas's changes, one line in the host), so the column's colour nodes grade the shader's output too — regardless of their place in the column (a test caveat, said in the law).
- No kit change is expected (the picture filter is untouched; the palette LUT is the kit's `toLUT`). If one appears, the clone road (BRIEF-COMMON.md).
