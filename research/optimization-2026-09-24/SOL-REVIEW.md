## 3. THE THREE HARD ONES

### a. Axial gas: interpolation against the fp16 write

Let `F(u)=j_l(z u)`, where `z` is the radial zero for one `(l,n_r)` row, `u=r/a∈[0,1]`, and `h=1/(N−1)`. Cubic Hermite with exact endpoint derivatives has the standard interval remainder

`|F(u) − H(u)| ≤ h⁴/384 · sup|F⁽⁴⁾| = z⁴ h⁴/384 · M₄(l,z)`,

where `M₄(l,z)=max_{0≤x≤z}|j_l⁽⁴⁾(x)|`. Linear interpolation has

`|F(u) − L(u)| ≤ h²/8 · sup|F″| = z² h²/8 · M₂(l,z)`.

Thus for an **absolute** tolerance `τ`, sufficient sample counts are `N ≥ 1 + (z⁴ M₄/(384τ))^(1/4)` for Hermite and `N ≥ 1 + (z² M₂/(8τ))^(1/2)` for linear. For a normal binary16 value `y` with exponent `e=floor(log₂|y|)`, half an ULP is `τ=2^(e−11)`; in the subnormal range it is `2⁻²⁵`. This bound must be evaluated for all 256 rows (l=0…15, nᵣ=0…15; z reaches ≈74), with removable x=0 limits handled. Then propagate row errors through the coefficient-weighted angular sum before the rgba16float write. It is not valid to infer a universal N from “11-bit mantissa ≈5e−4 relative”: fp16 ULP depends on exponent and subnormal range, and the final complex sum may sit arbitrarily close to a rounding midpoint [SOL-BRIEF.md:18–23; AUDIT-A.md:37–43,52–59; field.js:38–50,90–100,119–123].

A same-texel guarantee is stronger than `error < 0.5 ULP`: the final complex sum must stay on the same side of every binary16 rounding midpoint. A sound table proof therefore needs a bound on the accumulated mode error and each voxel’s distance to the nearest rounding midpoint, or exhaustive checked coverage of the shipped packed inputs. No reported sample count establishes that strict half-ULP/midpoint condition: `Hermite-256` measured max **1 fp16 ULP** from correctly-rounded f64 across 3,000 random voxels, versus shipped max **41**; it changes 10.7% of texel components vs shipped, and 0.010–0.017% of rendered pixels move exactly one 8-bit level. That is a strong approximation and a large speedup (112.6→8.1 ms FF, 70.45→8.08 ms Chromium), but not neutral under this run’s law. Linear needs ≥2048 samples for comparable one-ULP error; linear-256 is ~1% error [AUDIT-A.md:37–58].

The cheaper **exact-behavior** road is the audited FA2 memoization/early-out: same expressions and 0 texels changed, gas 112.6→45.1 ms. A recurrence in `l` cannot share the 16 radial nodes at fixed `l`: their `k`/zero values give different `x=k·r`. Upward recurrence is the candidate in the stable `x>l+1` region, but the existing helper documents its range only through `l≤12`; gas reaches `l=15`, so stability/accuracy for those rows needs direct validation. Miller remains the safe route in the difficult region, and changing recurrence/order is not a bitwise preservation proof [AUDIT-A.md:83–117; bessel.js:4–15; field.js:38–50; gas.js:16–23,43–51]. If approximate FA1 is accepted, build the radius-free table once, give each record stable row `l*16+nr`, and avoid combining lazy table build + launch into a 60–80 ms first-use hitch; worker/idle build and radius-drag behavior need measurement [REFUTE-A.md:25–29; REFUTE-F.md:75–85].

### b. Present pass: occupancy and specialization

The shader marches `ds` steps, samples `psiTex`, sets `rho=dot(s,s)/rhoMax`, uses `w=pow(rho,soft)`, and composites `a=1−exp(−4·sigma·stepN·wEff)`; default softness can be 0.3, and `sigma=24·exposure` [field.js:337–359,462–472]. A plausible exact-preserving *design to test* is a conservative brick maximum of normalized rho, with a one-texel halo for trilinear fetches; keep the original ray `t`, `ds`, jitter, and accumulation sequence, but bypass the texture read only when every contributing texel is proven zero. A DDA jump is not exact because it changes `t += ds` [AUDIT-A.md:232–234; field.js:348–355,472].

For an approximate cutoff `rho<ε`, cloud weight is at most `ε^soft`. Over a ray through the cube, `ΣstepN≤√3`, so omitted optical depth `ΔT≤4·sigma·√3·ε^soft`, and alpha error is bounded by `1−exp(−ΔT)`. With `ε=2⁻¹¹`, `ε^0.3≈0.102`; even at `soft=1`, `ΔT≈0.00338·sigma` (≈0.081 at default exposure). This is far above a universal 8-bit guarantee, and gamma/dither plus color accumulation make a small radiance bound insufficient: a pixel may lie arbitrarily near a quantization boundary. Shell, signed, phase and additive styles have different transfer functions, so a rho-only cutoff does not prove them neutral [field.js:341–342,358–392,413–429,445–483; AUDIT-A.md:232–234].
