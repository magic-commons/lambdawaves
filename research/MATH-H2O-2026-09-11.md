## ROUND 1 · SOL

**DERIVED-HERE · Integral choice.** Use McMurchie–Davidson (MD), not Obara–Saika (OS) or Rys, for the first Cartesian engine. MD makes one small Hermite-product table feed $S,T,V$, dipoles, and ERIs; the loops have static integer bounds and no root solver, which is the simplest JavaScript now and WGSL later. OS is attractive when shell-quartet vertical and horizontal recurrence is heavily optimised, and Rys is attractive at higher angular momentum, but both buy complexity that seven STO-3G functions cannot repay. This is an engineering choice, not a claim that MD is asymptotically fastest.

**KNOWN (McMurchie and Davidson, 1978, [J. Comput. Phys. 26, 218](https://doi.org/10.1016/0021-9991(78)90092-X)) · Hermite step.** For primitive Cartesian factors on one axis, set $p=\alpha+\beta$, $\mu=\alpha\beta/p$, $P=(\alpha A+\beta B)/p$, $\Lambda_t(q;p,P)=\partial_P^t e^{-p(q-P)^2}$, and
$$
(q-A)^i(q-B)^j e^{-\alpha(q-A)^2-\beta(q-B)^2}=\sum_{t=0}^{i+j}E_t^{ij}\Lambda_t.
$$
With out-of-range $E$ equal to zero,
$$
E_0^{00}=e^{-\mu(A-B)^2},\qquad
E_t^{i+1,j}=\frac{E_{t-1}^{ij}}{2p}+(P-A)E_t^{ij}+(t+1)E_{t+1}^{ij},
$$
$$
E_t^{i,j+1}=\frac{E_{t-1}^{ij}}{2p}+(P-B)E_t^{ij}+(t+1)E_{t+1}^{ij}.
$$
The three-axis product $E^{ab}_{tuv}=E^{a_xb_x}_tE^{a_yb_y}_uE^{a_zb_z}_v$ is the MD Hermite expansion table.

**KNOWN (McMurchie and Davidson, 1978) · One-electron horizontal assembly.** For unnormalised primitives,
$$
S_{ab}=E^{ab}_{000}\left(\frac{\pi}{p}\right)^{3/2},\qquad
M^{(q)}_{ab}=B_qS_{ab}+S_{a,b+\mathbf 1_q},
$$
and the kinetic integral is obtained from shifted overlaps,
$$
T_{ab}=-\frac12\sum_{q=x,y,z}\left[b_q(b_q-1)S_{a,b-2\mathbf1_q}-2\beta(2b_q+1)S_{ab}+4\beta^2S_{a,b+2\mathbf1_q}\right].
$$
Terms with negative angular momentum are zero. The dipole operator used by the kick is $-M^{(q)}$ for an electron; the stored position matrix is $M^{(q)}$.

**KNOWN (McMurchie and Davidson, 1978; Boys, 1950) · Vertical Coulomb step.** Define $R^n_{000}(\zeta,\mathbf R)=(-2\zeta)^nF_n(\zeta\lvert\mathbf R\rvert^2)$ and raise Hermite indices by
$$
R^n_{t+1,u,v}=tR^{n+1}_{t-1,u,v}+R_xR^{n+1}_{t,u,v},
$$
with the two cyclic analogues in $y,z$. Then the nuclear attraction is
$$
V_{ab}=-\sum_C Z_C\frac{2\pi}{p}\sum_{tuv}E^{ab}_{tuv}R^0_{tuv}(p,\mathbf P-\mathbf C).
$$
This is the vertical step: seed the required Boys orders at the top and recurse toward $n=0$ while raising $t,u,v$.

**KNOWN (McMurchie and Davidson, 1978) · Two-electron horizontal assembly.** For $q=\gamma+\delta$, $\mathbf Q=(\gamma\mathbf C+\delta\mathbf D)/q$, and $\rho=pq/(p+q)$,
$$
(ab\vert cd)=\frac{2\pi^{5/2}}{pq\sqrt{p+q}}
\sum_{tuv}\sum_{\tau\nu\phi}E^{ab}_{tuv}E^{cd}_{\tau\nu\phi}(-1)^{\tau+\nu+\phi}
R^0_{t+\tau,u+\nu,v+\phi}(\rho,\mathbf P-\mathbf Q).
$$
For general angular momentum, the horizontal transfer identity
$$
(a,b+\mathbf1_q\vert cd)=(a+\mathbf1_q,b\vert cd)+(A_q-B_q)(ab\vert cd)
$$
and its ket analogue move momentum between centres after the vertical auxiliary table is built. For $l\le1$, direct Hermite coefficients on both centres are smaller and should be used; the horizontal identity remains a test oracle.

**DERIVED-HERE · Contraction order.** Multiply each primitive result by its four contraction weights only after primitive normalisation, accumulate primitive pairs or quartets with compensated summation, then apply the eightfold ERI permutation symmetries when filling the dense tensor. Do not contract an already rounded $F_m$ table in `Float32`; CPU integrals and saved state remain `Float64`.

**KNOWN (Boys, 1950) · Boys definition and recursions.** Use
$$
F_m(t)=\int_0^1x^{2m}e^{-tx^2}\,dx,\qquad
F_m(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-t)^k}{k!(2m+2k+1)},\qquad
F_{m-1}(t)=\frac{2tF_m(t)+e^{-t}}{2m-1}.
$$

**DERIVED-HERE · Boys plan for $m\le4$.** For $0\le t<0.5$, evaluate the alternating series separately for every $m$, using the term update $a_{k+1}=a_k[-t/(k+1)](2m+2k+1)/(2m+2k+3)$ and compensated summation. For $0.5\le t<44$, seed $F_4$ with the positive Kummer-transformed series
$$
F_4(t)=\frac{e^{-t}}9\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^k}{(11/2)_k},
$$
then recurse downward through $F_{m-1}=[2tF_m+e^{-t}]/(2m-1)$. Stop either series only when the next term is below $2^{-55}$ times the accumulated magnitude, with a hard iteration guard.

**DERIVED-HERE · Boys asymptotic tail.** For $t\ge44$, use
$$
F_m(t)=\frac{(2m-1)!!\sqrt\pi}{2^{m+1}t^{m+1/2}}

### 1 · McMurchie–Davidson, proved

Throughout, one axis at a time: $p=\alpha+\beta$, $\mu=\alpha\beta/p$, $P=(\alpha A+\beta B)/p$, $x_P=q-P$, $X_{PA}=P-A$, $X_{AB}=A-B$, and $\Lambda_t(q)=\partial_P^{\,t}e^{-px_P^2}$ with $\Lambda_t\equiv0$ for $t<0$.

**Lemma 1 (Hermite shift).** DERIVED-HERE. $x_P\Lambda_t=\dfrac{1}{2p}\Lambda_{t+1}+t\,\Lambda_{t-1}$.
**Proof.** Induction on $t$. For $t=0$, $\Lambda_1=\partial_P e^{-p(q-P)^2}=2p\,x_P\Lambda_0$, which is the claim. Assume it at $t$ and apply $\partial_P$, using $\partial_P x_P=-1$ and $\partial_P\Lambda_s=\Lambda_{s+1}$: the left side gives $-\Lambda_t+x_P\Lambda_{t+1}$, the right side $\frac{1}{2p}\Lambda_{t+2}+t\Lambda_t$. Hence $x_P\Lambda_{t+1}=\frac{1}{2p}\Lambda_{t+2}+(t+1)\Lambda_t$. $\square$

**Proposition 1 (the two index-raising forms).** DERIVED-HERE, confirming Sol's Round 1 Hermite step verbatim. With $\Omega_{ij}=(q-A)^i(q-B)^je^{-\alpha(q-A)^2-\beta(q-B)^2}=\sum_tE_t^{ij}\Lambda_t$,
$$ E_0^{00}=e^{-\mu X_{AB}^2},\qquad E_t^{i+1,j}=\frac{E_{t-1}^{ij}}{2p}+X_{PA}E_t^{ij}+(t+1)E_{t+1}^{ij},\qquad E_t^{i,j+1}=\frac{E_{t-1}^{ij}}{2p}+X_{PB}E_t^{ij}+(t+1)E_{t+1}^{ij}. $$
**Proof.** The Gaussian product theorem gives $\Omega_{00}=e^{-\mu X_{AB}^2}e^{-px_P^2}=e^{-\mu X_{AB}^2}\Lambda_0$, which is the seed. Write $q-A=x_P+X_{PA}$, so $\Omega_{i+1,j}=(x_P+X_{PA})\Omega_{ij}$. Substituting Lemma 1,
$$ \Omega_{i+1,j}=\sum_tE_t^{ij}\Big[\tfrac{1}{2p}\Lambda_{t+1}+t\Lambda_{t-1}+X_{PA}\Lambda_t\Big], $$
and collecting the coefficient of $\Lambda_t$ — from $\Lambda_{t+1}$ at index $t-1$, from $\Lambda_{t-1}$ at index $t+1$ — gives the first recursion. Replacing $A$ by $B$ gives the second. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (Hermite moments).** DERIVED-HERE. $\int_{\mathbb R}\Lambda_t\,dq=\delta_{t0}\sqrt{\pi/p}$.
**Proof.** $\int\Lambda_t\,dq=\partial_P^{\,t}\int e^{-p(q-P)^2}dq=\partial_P^{\,t}\sqrt{\pi/p}$, a constant in $P$. $\square$

**Proposition 2 (overlap, dipole, kinetic).** DERIVED-HERE, confirming Sol's one-electron horizontal assembly including the sign pattern of $T$. For unnormalised primitives with Cartesian powers $a=(a_x,a_y,a_z)$ on $A$ and $b$ on $B$,
$$ S_{ab}=E^{ab}_{000}\Big(\frac{\pi}{p}\Big)^{3/2},\qquad M^{(q)}_{ab}=B_qS_{ab}+S_{a,b+\mathbf1_q},\qquad T_{ab}=-\tfrac12\sum_q\big[b_q(b_q-1)S_{a,b-2\mathbf1_q}-2\beta(2b_q+1)S_{ab}+4\beta^2S_{a,b+2\mathbf1_q}\big]. $$
**Proof.** The three-axis product $E^{ab}_{tuv}=E^{a_xb_x}_tE^{a_yb_y}_uE^{a_zb_z}_v$ and Lemma 2 on each axis kill every $t,u,v>0$, leaving $S_{ab}$. For the dipole, $q=(q-B_q)+B_q$, and multiplying the $b$ polynomial by $(q-B_q)$ is exactly $b_q\mapsto b_q+1$. For the kinetic energy, differentiate $G_j=(q-B)^je^{-\beta(q-B)^2}$ twice:
$$ \partial_q^2G_j=j(j-1)G_{j-2}-2\beta(2j+1)G_j+4\beta^2G_{j+2}, $$
since $\partial_qG_j=jG_{j-1}-2\beta G_{j+1}$ applied twice produces $-2\beta j$ and $-2\beta(j+1)$ on the $G_j$ term. Then $T_{ab}=-\tfrac12\sum_q\langle a\vert\partial_q^2\vert b\rangle$ is the stated combination of overlaps, all three axes sharing the same $E$ tables. Negative powers give zero by Lemma 2 and the convention $E^{ij}\equiv0$ out of range. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (Coulomb integral of a spherical Gaussian).** KNOWN (Boys 1950); proof recorded because the $2\pi/p$ of Sol's $V_{ab}$ is exactly this factor. $\displaystyle\int\frac{e^{-p\lvert \mathbf r-\mathbf P\rvert^2}}{\lvert\mathbf r-\mathbf C\rvert}d^3r=\frac{2\pi}{p}F_0(pR_{PC}^2)$, $R_{PC}=\lvert\mathbf P-\mathbf C\rvert$.
**Proof.** Use $\lvert\mathbf u\rvert^{-1}=\frac{2}{\sqrt\pi}\int_0^\infty e^{-s^2\lvert\mathbf u\rvert^2}ds$ and the Gaussian product theorem, giving $\frac{2}{\sqrt\pi}\int_0^\infty\big(\frac{\pi}{p+s^2}\big)^{3/2}e^{-\frac{ps^2}{p+s^2}R_{PC}^2}ds$. Substitute $x=s/\sqrt{p+s^2}$, so $ds=\sqrt p\,(1-x^2)^{-3/2}dx$ and $\frac{ps^2}{p+s^2}=px^2$; the $(1-x^2)^{3/2}$ factors cancel and the integral becomes $\frac{2}{\sqrt\pi}\frac{\pi^{3/2}}{p}\int_0^1e^{-pR_{PC}^2x^2}dx=\frac{2\pi}{p}F_0(pR_{PC}^2)$. $\square$

**Proposition 3 (vertical recursion and nuclear attraction).** DERIVED-HERE, confirming Sol's $R^n_{t+1,u,v}=tR^{n+1}_{t-1,u,v}+R_xR^{n+1}_{t,u,v}$ and the $-Z_C\,2\pi/p$ prefactor. $$ V_{ab}=-\sum_CZ_C\frac{2\pi}{p}\sum_{tuv}E^{ab}_{tuv}R^0_{tuv}(p,\mathbf P-\mathbf C). $$
**Proof.** $F_n'(T)=-F_{n+1}(T)$ from $F_n(T)=\int_0^1x^{2n}e^{-Tx^2}dx$, so $\partial_{R_x}\big[(-2\zeta)^nF_n(\zeta R^2)\big]=(-2\zeta)^{n+1}F_{n+1}\cdot R_x=R_xR^{n+1}_{000}$: the case $t=0$, where $\partial_{R_y},\partial_{R_z}$ do not touch $R_x$ and pass through. **Proposition 4 (two-electron assembly).** DERIVED-HERE, confirming both the $(-1)^{\tau+\nu+\phi}$ and the $2\pi^{5/2}/(pq\sqrt{p+q})$ of Sol's line. With $q=\gamma+\delta$, $\mathbf Q$ the second product centre and $\rho=pq/(p+q)$,
$$ (ab\vert cd)=\frac{2\pi^{5/2}}{pq\sqrt{p+q}}\sum_{tuv}\sum_{\tau\nu\phi}E^{ab}_{tuv}E^{cd}_{\tau\nu\phi}(-1)^{\tau+\nu+\phi}R^0_{t+\tau,u+\nu,v+\phi}(\rho,\mathbf P-\mathbf Q). $$
**Proof.** Change variables to $\mathbf u=\mathbf r_1-\mathbf r_2$ and $\mathbf w=(p\mathbf r_1+q\mathbf r_2)/(p+q)$, unit Jacobian, under which $p r_{1P}^2+q r_{2Q}^2=(p+q)\lvert\mathbf w-\mathbf W\rvert^2+\rho\lvert\mathbf u-(\mathbf P-\mathbf Q)\rvert^2$. **Proof.** $q-B_q=(q-A_q)+(A_q-B_q)$ inside the integrand. $\square$

### 3 · The Boys function: two of Sol's three regimes certified, the third replaced

**Proposition 6 (the Kummer seed is exact).** DERIVED-HERE, certifying Sol's $F_4(t)=\frac{e^{-t}}{9}\sum_k t^k/(11/2)_k$ and generalising it. For every $m\ge0$,
$$ F_m(t)=\frac{1}{2m+1}\,{}_1F_1\!\big(m+\tfrac12;m+\tfrac32;-t\big)=\frac{e^{-t}}{2m+1}\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{t^k}{(m+\tfrac32)_k}. $$
**Proof.** In $\sum_k\frac{(-t)^k}{k!(2m+2k+1)}$ write $\frac{1}{2m+2k+1}=\frac{1}{2m+1}\frac{(m+\frac12)_k}{(m+\frac32)_k}$, which is the identity $\frac{(a)_k}{(a+1)_k}=\frac{a}{a+k}$ with $a=m+\frac12$; that is the ${}_1F_1$. Kummer's transformation ${}_1F_1(a;b;z)=e^z\,{}_1F_1(b-a;b;-z)$ with $b-a=1$ and $(1)_k=k!$ gives the second form, whose terms are all positive. $\square$ At $m=4$ this is Sol's formula verbatim. **Proposition 7 (downward is the stable direction).** DERIVED-HERE. $F_{m-1}=\frac{2tF_m+e^{-t}}{2m-1}$ adds positive quantities, and the relative-error amplification is $\frac{\partial F_{m-1}}{\partial F_m}\frac{F_m}{F_{m-1}}=\frac{2tF_m}{2tF_m+e^{-t}}\le1$. **Proof.** Differentiate; both terms of the denominator are positive for $t\ge0$. $\square$

**Proposition 8 (the asymptotic tail, with a bound that decides the switch).** DERIVED-HERE. For $t>\frac{2m-1}{2}$,
$$ 0<\frac{(2m-1)!!\sqrt\pi}{2^{m+1}t^{m+1/2}}-F_m(t)=\int_1^\infty x^{2m}e^{-tx^2}dx\le\frac{e^{-t}}{2t}\Big(1-\frac{2m-1}{2t}\Big)^{-1}. $$
**Proof.** The difference is the tail of $\int_0^\infty$, whose value is the stated double factorial. **Lemma 4 (the Lorentzian-overlap bias of the damped sine transform).** DERIVED-HERE. Let $\operatorname{Im}\alpha(\omega)=\sum_kA_k\big[L_\gamma(\omega-\omega_k)-L_\gamma(\omega+\omega_k)\big]$ with $L_\gamma(x)=\gamma/(x^2+\gamma^2)$, $A_k=z_k^2$, $\gamma=1/\tau$, which is the exact $T\to\infty$ transform of $\delta\mu(t)=\sum_k2\kappa z_k^2\sin\omega_kt$. Then the local maximum near $\omega_j$ sits at $\omega_j+\delta_j$ with
$$ \delta_j=-\frac{\gamma^4}{A_j}\sum_{k\ne j}\frac{A_k}{(\omega_j-\omega_k)^3}+O(\gamma^6), $$
valid when $\lvert\omega_j-\omega_k\rvert\gg\gamma$ for all $k\ne j$.
**Proof.** $L_\gamma'(x)=-2\gamma x/(x^2+\gamma^2)^2$, so $L_\gamma'(\delta)=-2\delta/\gamma^3+O(\delta^3/\gamma^5)$ for $\lvert\delta\rvert\ll\gamma$ and $L_\gamma'(\Delta)=-2\gamma/\Delta^3+O(\gamma^3/\Delta^5)$ for $\lvert\Delta\rvert\gg\gamma$. Setting the derivative of the sum to zero at $\omega=\omega_j+\delta$ gives $-2A_j\delta/\gamma^3-2\gamma\sum_{k\ne j}A_k/(\omega_j-\omega_k)^3=0$. $\square$
**Corollary 4.1 (the peak reader's correction).** Adding $-\delta_j$ to a measured peak recovers the root to $O(\gamma^6)$, at the cost of knowing the other lines — so it is a second pass over a peak list, not a change to the transform. The bias scales as $\tau^{-4}$: doubling the run halves $\gamma$ and divides the bias by $16$.
