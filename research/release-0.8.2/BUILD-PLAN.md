## The shape (the law of this cut)
**THE WIGNER SLICE LEARNS THE OSCILLATOR.** `wigner.js` draws W(z, p_z), the cut of the six-dimensional Wigner function through the axis, for the hydrogenic
register; the oscillator's register (N ≤ 10, spherical) has the same structure — a finite superposition of products of a radial function and Y_lm — so the same
slice integral runs on it, and the closed forms are sharper: for the 1-D Fock states W_n(z, p) = (−1)ⁿ/π · e^{−(z² + p²)} L_n(2(z² + p²)), with W_1(0, 0) = −1/π
exactly, the coherent state a displaced Gaussian that is nowhere negative. The window's NEGATIVITY readout (∫|W| − 1 over the slice, already printed for hydrogen)
becomes the oracle: the oscillator's |0⟩ reads 0, |1⟩ along z reads its closed value, a slap on |0⟩ (a coherent state) reads 0 again — "this is what a classical
state looks like, and this is what a non-classical one looks like", said with numbers. THE COHERENCE MAP is the register seen as a density matrix: the N × N
picture of |c_a c_b*| over the populated labels (the off-diagonal elements are the coherences the beats live on; the equalizer's dephasing shrinks them — 0.6.0's
"this is what decoherence looks like" gets its picture), drawn as a small heat tile in METERS beside the beat equalizer's readouts, the palette's ramp, never
a new window. THE BEAD is the expectation point (⟨x⟩, ⟨y⟩, ⟨z⟩) of the state drawn on the stage as one bead with its trail (the dipole matrix the tree already
has: `dynamics.js` `angularDipoleZ` × `radialDipole`, rotated for x and y by the slap's rotors), the Ehrenfest motion made visible — a KEPLER-style overlay
through `toScreen`, on the skeleton's canvas (0.7.0 V2 made `#skeleton`; the bead shares it, drawn after the skeleton), off by default, PREFERENCE; and ψ ALONG
A RAY is CALCULUS's new tile: Re ψ, Im ψ and |ψ|² sampled along the ray from the origin through the bead (or along z when the bead is at the origin) at 128 points
from the register's closed forms (`psiAt`), drawn as a small line plot — the one-dimensional truth under the volume.
