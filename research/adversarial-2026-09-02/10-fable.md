### Lemma 2.1 (the fibre is RIGHT multiplication by $k$; DERIVED-HERE, MEASURED)
Write $u=u_1+u_2i+u_3j+u_4k$. Expanding $\mathrm{Ad}(u)k=uk\bar u$ gives $\big(2(u_2u_4+u_1u_3),\,2(u_3u_4-u_1u_2),\,u_1^2-u_2^2-u_3^2+u_4^2\big)$, and comparing with the lab's map,
$$\boxed{\;\mathrm{KS}(u)=A\,(uk\bar u),\qquad A=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\0&-1&0\\1&0&0\end{pmatrix}\in SO(3)\;}$$
(MEASURED $2.7\times10^{-16}$; $A$ is the half-turn about $(1,0,1)/\sqrt2$, $\det A=+1$). Hence $\mathrm{KS}(uz)=\mathrm{KS}(u)$ for every unit $z\in\mathbb C_k:=\mathrm{span}(1,k)$: **the KS/Hopf fibres are the orbits of right multiplication by $\mathbb C_k$, and the complex structure whose lines are fibre-tangent is $J=R_k:u\mapsto uk$**, $Ju=(-u_4,u_3,-u_2,u_1)$. MEASURED: $|d\mathrm{KS}(u)\cdot(uk)|\le8\times10^{-16}$ over 50 random $u$; the five other unit-imaginary multiplications ($ui,uj,iu,ju,ku$) fail at $4.7$–$6.5$. Not assumed — computed: the lab's KS map is a right-$k$ Hopf map, **not** the left-$i$ structure the holomorphy law in `rotor4.js` is written for.

### Theorem 2.2 (the slice is a cone over a circle whose axis is $n_+$ and whose half-angle is set by $n_-$; DERIVED-HERE, MEASURED)
Let $f_1=\bar q_Ljq_R$, $f_2=\bar q_Lkq_R$ (the visible frame), $P=\mathrm{span}(f_1,f_2)$, and $u=\rho\,\bar q_L v(t)q_R$ with $v(t)=\cos t\,j+\sin t\,k$. Put $w=\mathrm{Ad}(q_R)k$ and split $w=w_\parallel+w_i\,i$ with $w_\parallel\in\mathrm{span}(j,k)$. Since $\mathrm{Ad}(v)$ is the half-turn about the unit imaginary $v$, $\mathrm{Ad}(v)w=2(v\!\cdot\!w)v-w$, and in the $(j,k)$-plane with $w_\parallel=|w_\parallel|e^{it_0}$, $2(v\!\cdot\!w)v-w_\parallel=|w_\parallel|e^{i(2t-t_0)}$. Therefore
$$\mathrm{KS}\big(\rho\,\bar q_Lv(t)q_R\big)=\rho^2A\,\mathrm{Ad}(\bar q_L)\Big[\,|w_\parallel|\,e^{i(2t-t_0)}\big|_{(j,k)}-w_i\,i\,\Big].$$
Now $w_i=\langle i,\mathrm{Ad}(q_R)k\rangle=\langle\mathrm{Ad}(\bar q_R)i,k\rangle=-\,n_-\!\cdot\hat e_3$ and $\mathrm{Ad}(\bar q_L)i=n_+$ (the lab's `spherePoint`). Reading off:

### Corollary 2.3 (the posed corollary is REFUTED and replaced)
*As posed:* "a holomorphic drag (minus rotor only) never changes whether the slice is degenerate." **REFUTED.** The lab's holomorphy law (`isHolomorphic`) is for $J_{\rm lab}=L_i$ (left multiplication by $i$), whose complex lines are $\{n_+=\pm\hat e_1\}\times S^2_-$ — the minus-rotor drive keeps $n_+$ and roams $S^2_-$. The KS fibre structure is $J=R_k$, whose complex lines are $S^2_+\times\{n_-=\pm\hat e_3\}$. The two structures commute ($L_iR_k=R_kL_i$) and live on opposite factors. MEASURED from a degenerate rotor ($n_-=\hat e_3$): a minus-rotor drive by $\theta$ about $y$ gives $\sigma_2/\sigma_1=\sin\theta$ exactly ($0.099833,\,0.479426,\,0.841471$ at $\theta=0.1,0.5,1$; $n_{-z}=\cos\theta$) — the holomorphic drag switches the degeneracy **off**; a plus-rotor drive by the same angles leaves $\sigma_2/\sigma_1\le10^{-8}$ and the image a ray while $n_+$ wanders. *Replacement:* **the PLUS-rotor drag never changes whether the KS slice is degenerate; the MINUS-rotor drag is exactly the control that does.** In the instrument: HOLO mode (minus rotor) is the mode in which the KS picture can fold flat and unfold; the plus rotor turns the cone's axis $A\,n_+$ without touching its opening angle. That is a better dial than the one the claim would have given: one sphere for the axis, the other for the aperture — the same $S^2\times S^2$ split as Theorem B.1, now read on the KS screen.
